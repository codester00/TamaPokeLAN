# TamaPoke v4.12

The first release since this fork began. The first half of these notes is how
the game stood when the fork started: the last published release notes,
unchanged. After the line is everything that has changed since, described as
it works today in v4.12. Things that were added and later changed again are
described only in their final form.

## Before you update

- **Back up first.** "Backup, then install" on the installer page does it for
  you, and checks the backup before saying it worked.
- **Leave "Erase device" unchecked.** It is the one thing that wipes a save.
  Your creature, party, box, bag, badges and Pokedex all carry over.
- **Black screen after installing?** The board is usually waiting in its
  download mode, not broken. Hold PWR for about 10 seconds to switch it fully
  off, then press it again.
- **Do not go back to an older version afterwards.** Saves have grown a lot
  since the fork (creature IDs, Bill's PC, new records), and older versions
  cannot read them: going back empties the party and the box at the least.
  Keep your backup if you might.
- **LAN battles need v4.12 on both boards.** Trading works with v4.08 and
  later. An older board refuses the link rather than get anything wrong.
- **Your old box moves into Bill's PC** the first time the board starts.

# Part 1 -- The game when the fork began

The fork started from the last release, v3.28 (plus a few edits that were
never released; see the end of this part). There are no release notes before
v3.20. At that point the game had:

- All 1025 species and their shinies, drawn from the microSD card (Galar and
  Paldea had no sprite pack yet, so they read NEEDS PACK).
- The full life cycle: an egg chosen by rarity, care, evolution, and three
  endings (farewell, release, runaway); a bred Pokedex with a gallery.
- A starter choice by region at first boot, and a choice of which region your
  eggs come from.
- Turn-based battles against trainers, wild creatures and, over LAN, a friend;
  gym ladders and Elite Fours for seven regions on easy and hard; wild capture
  and a shared bag; IVs and training.
- A daily streak, bond, medals and nicknames; backgrounds by biome and real
  time of day; the ball minigame and the punching bag; an animated bath.
- Real-time ageing through the board's clock while it is off; battery and PWR
  button support; dimming; sound; six languages.
- The Poke Mart with a step counter, LAN battles with win/loss records, and
  the web installer with backups.

The release notes from that time follow.

## v3.28

Installer-only release. The firmware itself is unchanged from v3.27 except for the version number.

## Web installer

- **Fixed a real stall**: "Backup, then install" used to try to click the actual install button for you once the backup finished. That click happened too late for the browser to still count it as a real gesture, so the install dialog silently never opened -- no error, nothing logged, just nothing happening. It's now an explicit two-step flow: "Backup, then install" reads the save, downloads it, then greys out and says "Backup complete, now →", pointing at a second "Install update" button that starts the actual flash with a real click.
- "Install without backup" is still there as a smaller link, for anyone who wants to skip straight to flashing without connecting for a backup first.
- The "Backup, then install" and "Connect board" buttons are green now, matching the firmware's own UI, instead of black.
- The install dialog itself now says **"Install TamaPoke v3.28"** instead of a bare "Install TamaPoke" that gave no hint which build was about to be flashed.

Existing saves are unaffected by any of this.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/reallyjustsomeguy/TamaPokeLANExp/compare/v3.27...v3.28

## v3.27

Two bugs found by hand, fixed and verified against the full 41-suite emulator run.

## Fixed

- **Victory music kept playing after a wild encounter.** Beating or catching a wild Pokémon plays victory music, but dismissing that result and returning to the main screen never stopped it — the trainer/gym win screen has its own dismiss path that correctly silences the music, but a wild encounter's simpler message-dismiss never went through it. The music now stops on the same tap that closes the battle screen, whether you caught something, just won, or picked up an item drop along the way.
- **LAN battle names could, in principle, get crossed under packet loss.** Tracing the pairing handshake turned up a real bug: your own name and your opponent's name shared a single field internally, and a resend during a slow or lossy pairing could echo your opponent's name back to them instead of sending your own. In practice this never showed up — a normal pairing finishes before the resend timer that triggers it ever fires, which is why testing it repeatedly never caught anything — but it's fixed properly now: your own name and the name you display for your opponent are two separate fields that can no longer cross-contaminate.

## Also

- A regression test (`focus_test.cpp`) was asserting the *old* behavior for a total save-write failure during a party swap — that the incoming Pokémon stayed live in memory for a later save to "catch up" on. The actual code rolls back to whichever Pokémon was out before instead, which turns out to be the more correct design: leaving the incoming one live without committing the party slot would just be the same duplicate-Pokémon bug pointed the other direction. The test (and the doc note describing it) now match what the code actually does.

Existing saves are unaffected by any of this.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/reallyjustsomeguy/TamaPokeLANExp/compare/v3.26...v3.27

## v3.26

Real two-player battles, for the first time — and three bugs found running exactly that for the first time on real hardware.

## LAN battles

Pair two boards over WiFi and battle head-to-head from the gym ladder's **LAN BATTLE** button. Bring whichever team you like; there's no level or team-size cap like the gym ladders have, since you already know what your opponent is bringing. A running **win/loss record** is kept against each rival and shown the next time you two pair up.

Three real bugs turned up during that first session and are fixed here:

- Swapping which Pokémon you were raising could, if a save happened to fail at the wrong moment, leave the same Pokémon showing up in two places at once — on your team and still on the main screen. Saves are now retried automatically, and if one truly can't go through, the swap is rolled back cleanly instead of leaving anything duplicated or lost.
- A failing save could retry hundreds of times a second instead of backing off, which was hard on the flash storage for no benefit.
- Dismissing a message after your opponent quit mid-battle could crash the game. Fixed.

Also: a battle no longer drops just because you took a while deciding a move, and a **QUIT** option appears if you've genuinely been waiting a long time for the other side.

## The Poké Mart

Real steps, tracked by a pedometer, turn into Pokédollars — 1 step = $1, capped at $999,999 (the same ceiling the real games use). Spend them on Poké Balls, Potions and vitamins at a new stop between EXPLORE and the gyms.

## Also fixed

- A save could fail and then silently keep failing afterward with nothing telling you anything was wrong. It now recovers automatically the next time it succeeds.
- The serial/debug console showed some messages in Spanish; they're now consistently in English.

Existing saves are unaffected by any of this — nothing here changes how a save is stored.

## Installer

- Published rebuilt v3.26 firmware and an updated web-installer manifest. Existing NVS saves remain outside the installer write ranges.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/reallyjustsomeguy/TamaPokeLANExp/compare/v3.22...v3.26

## v3.22

Backups on the [installer page](https://eperdeme.github.io/TamaPoke/web/) now happen as part of flashing instead of being something you have to remember, and they are checked before you are told you have one.

## Back up, then install

- The installer has a new **Back up, then install** button, and it is the primary one. It reads the save off the board, verifies it, downloads it as a `.tpsave` file and keeps a copy in your browser, and only then hands over to the flasher. **Install without backup** is still there if you want it.
- Your browser may ask you to choose the board twice: once to read the save, once to flash. That is a limitation of how the two connections work and cannot be avoided from the page.
- A brand-new board, or one sitting in download mode, has no save to read. The page says so and goes straight through to the flasher rather than getting in the way.

## Backups are now verified, not just downloaded

- **A backup is checked before you are told it worked.** The board reports how many bytes it produced; the page now compares that against what actually arrived and verifies the save's checksum. Previously a capture was accepted if it merely ended with the right line, so a read that stopped early could be downloaded and presented as a complete backup. A partial backup is worse than no backup, because you only find out when you need it.
- Restoring runs the same check before anything is sent, so a truncated or corrupted file is refused up front with a reason rather than after the whole upload.
- Restoring now waits for the board to acknowledge every chunk instead of assuming a fixed delay was long enough.

## Recent backups, kept in this browser

- The Save vault now lists your recent backups with the date, trainer name and creature, and each one can be restored to a connected board or downloaded again in one click. Twelve are kept.
- **This is a convenience, not a backup, and the page says so.** They live only in that browser on that computer, and are lost if you clear site data, use private browsing, or move to another machine. The downloaded `.tpsave` file is the real backup.
- Nothing is uploaded anywhere. There is no account, no server and no copy of your save outside your own machine, which is deliberate.

## Firmware

- `STATS` now reports a `board=` line, taken from the chip's own hardware ID. It exists so the installer can tell two devices' backups apart in that list. It identifies nothing else and authorises nothing.

## Under the hood

- The page's save-handling code has tests for the first time, run against a save exported by the real firmware. The checksum is pinned to the published check value for its algorithm rather than to a second copy of it.
- The page could previously hold the serial port open after it believed it had disconnected, which would have stopped the flasher claiming the device. Fixed, and it is what makes the backup-then-install handoff work.
- The `.js` cache keys the page uses to defeat CDN caching are now computed at build time instead of being typed by hand, and a release is refused if they have drifted.

## Installer

- Published rebuilt v3.22 firmware and an updated web-installer manifest. Existing NVS saves remain outside the installer write ranges.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/compare/v3.21...v3.22

## v3.21

A hardening release. **Nothing behaves differently, and there is no bug here that anyone could have hit** — if you are on v3.20 there is no urgency in taking it.

It closes the last remaining instance of the fault behind [#4](https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/issues/4), found while writing that fix up rather than reported.

## Gym region handling

- The team-select screen now remembers which region's gym it was opened for. It previously read the region from the gym menu every time it needed it, so the hard-mode team-size limit and the leader named at the top of the screen were answered from wherever the menu happened to be pointing rather than from the gym you had actually chosen.
- Confirming a fight now explicitly fights the region the team-select screen showed you, so the team limit you were given, the leader you were shown and the ladder the badge is filed under cannot disagree.

This was **not reachable in v3.20**. Every gesture that could move the gym menu already checks for the team-select screen first and backs out of it instead, so the menu could not move underneath it. The fix is here because that protection is an ordering that has to keep being true, and this is the same missing-snapshot mistake that put Brock's Onix into every region's gym — one screen earlier, and one dispatch-order change away from mattering.

A regression test now opens the picker on one region's gym, moves the menu underneath it to a region whose first gym fields a different number of creatures, and asserts the limit does not follow. It picks that second region by searching for one that actually differs instead of naming it, because the obvious pair (Johto and Kalos) both field two and made the test pass whether the fix was present or not.

## Installer

- Published rebuilt v3.21 firmware and an updated web-installer manifest. Existing NVS saves remain outside the installer write ranges.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/compare/v3.20...v3.21

## v3.20

Fixes [#3](https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/issues/3) and [#4](https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/issues/4).

## Gym ladders outside Kanto ([#4](https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/issues/4))

- **Every gym leader now sends out their own team.** In Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova, Kalos and Alola, the leader opened with the correct creature but the moment you knocked it out the fight switched to Brock and his Onix. The battle kept a note of which region it belonged to and then never filled it in, so it fell back to Kanto for everything after the first creature.
- **Badges earned outside Kanto are now recorded against the right region.** The same missing note sent the badge to Kanto's ladder, which is why the next gym in your own region stayed locked and the other regions could not be progressed at all.

Badges you have already earned are unaffected. Any that were misfiled to Kanto stay where they were put, so a non-Kanto ladder may still show a gap you need to re-beat; the leader will now award it correctly.

## Save integrity ([#3](https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/issues/3))

- Saves are now written as complete, checksummed snapshots instead of around fifty separate values. A power cut, a four-second power-button hold, or one failed write partway through a save could previously leave some values updated and others not, so a creature could come back with its current Attack training beside its older Defence, Speed and species. The device now loads the newest complete snapshot and falls back to the previous one if the last write was interrupted.
- The same protection now covers **player progress**: badges, the Pokedex, the daily streak, medal totals and minigame records. A cut partway through those could leave a gym badge won with no Pokedex entry for the creature that earned it, or a medal counted on the creature but missing from your lifetime total.
- **A retired creature waiting for a party slot is no longer lost if the device turns off before you choose one.** The farewell handed the creature over in memory only and the party slot was not written until you accepted it, so powering off during the banner, or while the party screen sat open, left you with the new egg and nothing else. It is now stored together with the egg, so the two survive or fail as one.
- A save that cannot be completed now leaves the previous save untouched and retries, instead of applying half of itself. Write failures were previously ignored.

Upgrading is recommended for anyone who uses the power button to switch the device off, and strongly recommended for anyone who has seen a creature's species, training or badges change after a reboot, or who plays the gyms outside Kanto.

Existing saves are carried over automatically on first boot. Nothing is reset, and no action is needed.

## Save diagnostics

- A warning triangle now appears beside the battery if saving has stopped working, meaning flash storage could not be opened or three writes in a row have failed. There was previously no sign of this on screen at all. If it appears, back the save up with `EXPORT` over the serial console while you still can.
- A pending save is now flushed when you begin holding the power button and when the battery reaches its low-battery warning, rather than only on a timer.
- `HEALTH` over the serial console now reports whether saving is working and how much flash storage is left.

## Installer

- Published rebuilt v3.20 firmware and an updated web-installer manifest. Existing NVS saves remain outside the installer write ranges.

**Full changelog**: https://github.com/eperdeme/TamaPoke/compare/v3.19...v3.20

## Unreleased edits before the fork

Between v3.28 and the fork a set of edits was made that never became a
release: a rework of creature weight, critical hits as in the newer games,
Steel types immune to poison, and a smarter battle AI. As made, they left the
firmware unable to build. They were repaired and kept, and they ship for the
first time in this release (see Part 2).

# Part 2 -- Everything new since the fork, as it stands in v4.12

## Finding your way around

- **The screens are in a new order.** Swipe left from your creature for your
  party, the Poke Mart, the Pokedex and your player card. Swipe right for
  Explore, the gyms and the **PLAZA**. Your own things are on the left,
  battles on the right.
- **Swipe down is always BACK**, one level. Swipe up opens your creature's
  card. Dragging along the edge of the screen turns pages on any screen that
  has them, and a swipe that heads across the screen always moves you to the
  next screen, even if it starts near the edge.
- **BACK buttons** are on every Pokedex screen, every Battle Zone screen and
  the Battle Hall, and they are taller and harder to miss.
- **A tap always goes to the screen you can see.** A move offer waits until
  you close whatever you had open.
- **19 trainer avatars.** Tap the avatar on your player card to choose one.

## Caring for your creature

- **Needs fall slowly while awake**, and the **8-hour rule**: get all four
  needs to 90 or more and you can leave for 8 hours. It will be hungry and
  tired when you come back, but it is not punished and cannot run away. Only
  getting all four back above 90 starts a fresh 8 hours; a single snack does
  not.
- **Sleep follows your day.** Put to bed at night, it sleeps in until 8:00.
  From 8:00 to 18:00 a sleeping creature gets up once its energy is full, so
  putting it to bed in the day is a nap. You can always wake it yourself.
  Switching the screen off at night puts it to bed too.
- **Petting wears off.** Each pet gives less joy than the last until you play
  one of the joy and bond games.
- **Fitness and real weight on the card.** The card shows FITNESS and a weight
  in kilograms, based on the species' real Pokedex weight.
- **Gender.** Every creature is male or female (or neither, for the species
  that are), shown on its card. Species that are always one gender are that
  gender.
- **Every creature has its own ID**, so trades and the PC always know exactly
  which one is which.
- **Shiny eggs are rare.** A farewell egg is shiny about one time in a
  hundred, and the best care (a long streak and a strong bond) makes that up to
  four times likelier. Eggs from other endings are half as likely. Wild
  shinies are 1 in 4096.
- **The level bar reads MAX LEVEL at 100** instead of counting to 101.

## Training

- **Training is a list of games.** Tap the dumbbell and pick one; each row
  says what it trains and how far along you are.
  - Punching Bag (strength), Reaction Test (speed), Ball Game (defence)
  - Rock Smash (strength + speed), Block! (defence + speed), Tilt Maze (speed
    + defence), Boulder Push (strength + defence)
  - Berry Catch, Memory and Hide and Seek raise joy and bond instead.
- **Training costs what it pays.** A full session costs 12 energy and 5 food,
  a smaller one less, and a round that earned nothing costs nothing. Under 15
  energy a creature is **TOO TIRED** to train; the joy games stay open.
- **Every result screen says what you got**, and a zero says why: already at
  MAX, JOY MAX, or BOND MAX TODAY (bond has a daily limit).
- **Training counts more as a creature grows.** In battle, training adds in
  proportion to level, like IVs do. The training screen still shows 0 to 100;
  the card shows the creature's real battle numbers.
- **Vitamins give +10** and can go to any party member: the bag asks who.

## Evolution

- **Evolving teaches the moves the new form has already passed**, so a late
  evolution no longer leaves a creature with empty move slots.
- **Evolution stones**: Fire, Water, Thunder, Leaf, Moon, Sun, Shiny, Dusk,
  Dawn and Ice Stones ($2,100 at the Mart), and the Black Augurite ($3,000).
  Use one from the bag. Species that evolve by stone in the games only evolve
  by stone here. Stones are also found after wild wins in the places the games
  hide them, about one win in 25 there.
- **Eevee** evolves by its five stones, or, once its bond is 40 and it is at
  least two days old, into Espeon by day, Umbreon by night, or Sylveon if it
  knows a Fairy move.
- **Branches without items**: Tyrogue by its Attack and Defense, Wurmple and
  Cosmoem by the creature itself (Cosmoem becomes whichever of Solgaleo and
  Lunala you do not own yet), and Applin and Charcadet let you choose. Some
  evolutions depend on gender (Burmy, Combee, Salandit, and the Dawn Stone for
  Kirlia and Snorunt).
- **Shedinja**: when Nincada evolves, a Shedinja joins you too if you have a
  spare Poke Ball and room.
- **Meltan** evolves after 10,000 steps walked while you raise it.
- **Trade evolution.** Creatures that evolve by trade arrive marked and evolve
  the next time you raise them. Playing alone, the **Linking Cord** ($9,800)
  does the same for the creature you are raising, including Karrablast and
  Shelmet with a partner from your party.
- **Trade-only evolvers get their farewell.** A Kadabra that will never be
  traded is no longer stuck waiting forever.
- **Second evolutions come ten levels after the first** where the games had
  them too close: Ursaluna 40, Mamoswine 43, Kingambit 62, Azumarill 35,
  Blissey 40, Hydrapple 40.

## Explore

- **Every region has real areas from the games.** Kanto 36, Johto 35, Hoenn
  43, Sinnoh 54, Unova 43, Kalos 30, Alola 54, Galar 39 and Paldea 32, with the
  games' own creatures and odds. Pick a region, then an area.
- **Areas open with badges.** A locked row says what it needs, for example
  REQUIRES BOULDER BADGE. A badge from either difficulty counts. Some of what
  an area offers arrives as your journey moves on, the way it did in the games.
- **Levels follow the gym ladder**, not your creature. Hard mode means a
  smarter opponent and better drops, not higher levels.
- **Day and night** in Johto, Sinnoh and Paldea; Headbutt trees, rustling
  grass, dust clouds and SOS calls where the games had them; **Galar's
  weather** changes daily and shows on the area's row.
- **Legendaries live where they did in the games.** Your first visit after the
  right badge guarantees a meeting; after that they turn up now and then until
  caught. Some roam their region. A star on a row means a guaranteed meeting is
  waiting. Story legendaries no longer hatch from eggs; mythicals still do.
  Necrozma appears only once all seven Ultra Beasts are caught.
- **One-off encounters**, marked with `!` while due: the sleeping Snorlax, the
  Power Plant's Electrode, Sudowoodo, Lapras on Fridays, the red Gyarados, and
  Kecleon. Missed ones come back now and then until caught.
- **Swarms**: some days an area has a swarm, shown on its row.
- **Safari Zones** in Kanto, Hoenn and Sinnoh's Great Marsh, each with its own
  game's rules: no team, 30 Safari Balls, and bait, rocks, Pokeblocks or mud
  to tempt the creature closer.
- **Wild creatures have varied movesets**, and a small ball on the foe's plate
  shows a species you already own.
- **Drops**: one item after a win, sometimes two (more often on hard), plus
  the chance of a TM, a stone or an Ether.
- An area whose creatures have no art yet reads **NO VALID SPRITES**; one whose
  sprite pack is missing reads **NEEDS PACK**.

## Battles

- **About 250 moves**, including 150 new ones, so every creature has attacks
  at the right levels. Many attacks also change stats, as in the games.
- **PP.** Every move has its real PP. A full night's sleep refills it. Out of
  PP, a creature uses Struggle.
- **Two-turn moves**: Dig, Fly, Bounce, Dive and Shadow Force take the user out
  of reach for a turn; only the moves that reach it still hit (Earthquake on
  Dig, Gust and Thunder on Fly, Surf on Dive).
- **SKETCH** (Smeargle) copies the foe's last move for good, and **TRANSFORM**
  (every Ditto) becomes the foe for the fight, picture and all.
- **Critical hits** work as in the newer games, and Steel types cannot be
  poisoned.
- **You choose who comes on after a faint.** Against a trainer you are told
  what they are sending next and may switch first for free. Hard trainers pick
  their next creature to suit yours.
- **The team picker sets the battle order** in the order you tap, and
  remembers your team next time.
- **Battles read like the games**: HP falls with the line that caused it, a
  creature faints with its own line, poison and burn damage get their own
  lines, and a gym win plays its last turn before the badge screen.
- **The battle bag pages** through everything usable. **X items** raise a stat
  sharply for the fight.
- **Running from a trainer is a loss**, after asking. Losing a gym or League
  battle ends with Mom's blackout screen. There is no penalty.
- **Indoor backgrounds** for gyms, the Elite Four, champions and LAN battles,
  and **real Game Boy music**: wild, trainer, gym leader and champion themes,
  victory jingles, the Game Corner and more.

## Gyms and the League

- **Nine gym ladders**: Galar (with the Champion Cup) and Paldea join the seven
  regions. The gym list remembers whether you were on easy or hard.
- **The League is one run.** Beat the first Elite Four member and the run
  starts: HP comes back for every fight, but PP does not, and you cannot put
  your creature to bed. Lose, give up or take more than an hour and the run
  ends; badges won are kept.
- **Hard mode is tuned leader by leader.** A team built for the matchup wins
  almost every time; an ordinary one about a third of the time at a gym and
  about one time in ten against the Elite Four and champions.
- **Prize money**: every trainer win pays, as in the games. Easy pays half of
  hard, and a rematch pays half again.

## Money, the Mart and items

- **Steps are money**: $1 a step, up to $999,999.
- **Sell at the Mart** for half the price. Tap the title to switch BUY and
  SELL.
- **TMs are items.** Owned once, a TM teaches any of your creatures, at any
  level. The Mart sells five a day, different each day; TMs also drop in the
  wild and come from the Game Corners and the Battle Zone.
- **PP Up** ($10,000) gives a move a fifth more PP, up to three times. It stays
  with the creature, through trades and LAN.
- **ETHER** gives a move 10 PP back. A rare wild find.

## Party, Bill's PC and the Pokedex

- **Bill's PC** holds up to six of every species. Open it from the middle of
  the party ring, pick a region, and creatures are grouped by species. **GO TO
  #** jumps to a number, and **MISSING ONLY** shows what you still lack.
- **TO PC** on a party creature's card stores it. Tapping a creature only
  looks at it.
- **The Pokedex shows what you have**: a filled entry means obtained, a green
  dot means fully registered, and each region shows how many you own.
- Every creature you catch, win or hatch goes to the party, then the PC.

## Trading

- **Trade one for one** with another board from the PLAZA's **LAN TRADE**.
  Pick from your party or straight from Bill's PC. Each creature's card shows
  first, with TRADE and BACK; nothing is offered until you tap TRADE.
- **Nothing is ever lost or duplicated.** If the connection drops mid-trade,
  both boards keep trying for 5 minutes. After that each screen says what it
  holds, and the two of you choose REVERT or KEEP.

## LAN battles

- **LAN BATTLE is on the PLAZA.** Both boards see the full battle story, PP
  counts, each player picks their own next creature after a faint, and leaving
  a battle counts as a forfeit (a rival who stops answering does not).

## The Game Corners and the PLAZA

- **Celadon Game Corner** (Kanto, after Lt. Surge) and **Goldenrod Game
  Corner** (Johto, after Bugsy) appear in Explore's area lists. Buy coins with
  step money (50 for $1,000), play the original slot machines (Red's at
  Celadon, Crystal's at Goldenrod, lucky machines and all) and card flip at
  Goldenrod, and trade coins for creatures and TMs at the prize counters.
- **Something is waiting on the Kanto coast** for a certain electric mouse
  that has learned to ride the waves.
- **The Battle Hall** (PLAZA, open to everyone): your creature alone against
  all 18 types, toughest matchups first. Each battle costs energy and pays a
  little money; the run is saved between battles. Clear all 18 and your
  creature is a HALL CHAMPION for good.
- **The Battle Zone** (PLAZA, after every region's champion): win Battle
  Points (BP) in the **gauntlet** (daily or custom rules, a boss every 20th
  challenger, the daily run's first win doubles its BP) and the
  **tournament** (16 trainers from every region, full teams of six at level
  100). Entry is $1,500 and $2,500. Spend BP at the prize counter: Master
  Ball, PP Up, PP MAX, BOTTLE CAP, vitamins, stones, the Linking Cord,
  signature-move TMs and five more TMs a day. Symbols on your player card mark
  a tournament win and long gauntlet streaks.

## Minigames

- Tilt Maze rolls the right way, Boulder Push's needle is slower, Rock Smash
  waits for a frozen screen, the Memory game waits for you, and card flip's
  result is visible.

## Saves, backups and the installer

- **Backups hold everything**, Bill's PC included, and arrive whole even on
  boards whose USB connection drops output.
